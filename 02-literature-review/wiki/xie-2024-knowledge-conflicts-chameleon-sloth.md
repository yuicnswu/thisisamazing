**APA:** Xie, J., Zhang, K., Chen, J., Lou, R., & Su, Y. (2024). Adaptive chameleon or stubborn sloth: Revealing the behavior of large language models in knowledge conflicts. In *International Conference on Learning Representations*.

## 1. Research question
How receptive are LLMs to external evidence that conflicts with their parametric memory?

## 2. Limitation of existing methods
Earlier conflict studies used heuristic entity substitution, which produces incoherent evidence and may understate how much models follow context.

## 3. Contributions
A framework to elicit a model's memory and construct coherent counter-memory evidence; a controlled study of single-source and mixed-evidence settings.

## 4. Proposed methods
Elicit each model's answer, generate coherent counter-memory passages, and test responses when evidence is alone, mixed with supporting evidence, or varied in order and quantity.

## 5. Key findings
LLMs are highly receptive to coherent counter-memory evidence, but show strong confirmation bias toward their memory when evidence is mixed.

## 6. Limitations
QA only; evidence is always present, never missing or vague.
